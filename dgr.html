Aviation Safety Standard

Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR)

Customer/Consignor/Sender agrees not to ship prohibited and restricted items. If shipped, the sender is fully responsible and liable for statutory penalties enforced by TCOI, Customs, airlines, or aviation safety agencies.

Mandatory Rule: The IATA DGR is updated annually and legally enforced worldwide under ICAO / UN civil aviation mandates.

Scope & Shippers Responsibilities

📦

Classification & Pack

Shippers must ensure precise UN specification packaging, UN labels, and accurate dangerous goods declarations.

⛔

Forbidden / Hidden

Concealed, misdeclared, or unregistered dangerous goods carry heavy regulatory penalties and forfeiture.

✉️

Postal Limitations

Postal networks prohibit almost all dangerous items with narrow exceptions for biological specimens or dry ice.

IATA 9 Dangerous Goods Hazard Classes

International air transport hazard classification system

9 UN Classes
💣
Class 1

Explosives

Fireworks, ammo, flares. Risk of detonation.

🧯
Class 2

Gases

Flammable & non-toxic compressed cylinders.

🛢️
Class 3

Flammable Liquids

Paints, solvents, fuels, alcohol, adhesives.

🔥
Class 4

Flammable Solids

Matches, sulfur, phosphorus, reactive metals.

🧪
Class 5

Oxidizers & Peroxides

Bleach, nitrates. Supply oxygen to burn.

☠️
Class 6

Toxic & Infectious

Poisons, pesticides, clinical bio-samples.

📡
Class 7

Radioactive Material

Medical isotopes, industrial radiography.

🧫
Class 8

Corrosives

Battery acid, caustic soda, corrosive fluids.

🔋
Class 9

Miscellaneous (Lithium)

Lithium ion batteries, dry ice, magnetized cargo.

Mandatory Staff Training Compliance

All logistics staff preparing, receiving, or handling air cargo must hold active IATA DGR certification. Non-compliance results in immediate air consignment rejection and regulatory fines.
